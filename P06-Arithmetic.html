Arithmetic operators

Arithmetic operators perform calculations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication and division.

PurposeSyntaxExamplesPractice & test

1 · Purpose

Arithmetic expressions combine values and variables to calculate results. Correct operator selection, precedence and data types are essential: a mathematically plausible expression can still implement the wrong algorithm.

Core principle: * and / have higher precedence than + and −; use parentheses to make complex expressions explicit.

2 · Syntax and worked examples

Formal syntax

+  -  *  /  DIV  MOD  (parentheses)
  • * and / have higher precedence than + and −; use parentheses to make complex expressions explicit.
  • / produces a REAL result, even when both operands are integers.
  • DIV gives the integer quotient; MOD gives the remainder.
  • Check for division by zero and ensure the chosen type can represent the result.

Worked examples with reasoning

Worked example 1 · Precedence
Result ← 2 + 3 * 4
OUTPUT Result

Multiplication first: 3 * 4 = 12, then 2 + 12 = 14.

Worked example 2 · Integer quotient and remainder
Quotient ← 17 DIV 5
Remainder ← 17 MOD 5
OUTPUT Quotient, Remainder

17 divided by 5 gives quotient 3 and remainder 2.

Worked example 3 · Convert minutes to hours and minutes
Hours ← TotalMinutes DIV 60
Minutes ← TotalMinutes MOD 60

For 135 minutes, Hours is 2 and Minutes is 15. DIV and MOD work together for decomposition.

Common examination errors

  • Assuming 2 + 3 * 4 equals 20 without parentheses.
  • Using / when an integer quotient is required, or DIV when a fractional result is needed.
  • Forgetting that MOD returns a remainder, not a quotient.
  • Dividing by a value that may be zero.
